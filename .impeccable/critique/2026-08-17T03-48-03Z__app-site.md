## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment.** Listing and article surfaces: yes, reads as generated. Portal SSO: no, reads as authored.

Tells on the site surfaces: gradient-clipped text heading at `app/site/page.tsx:86-91` (`WebkitBackgroundClip: text` + `WebkitTextFillColor: transparent` with no color fallback — an absolute ban and the most recognizable generated-UI signature); two full carousel implementations (`HeroSection.tsx` 605 lines, `FullscreenHero.tsx` 210 lines) with divergent indicator sizes and aria coverage; four stacked decorative devices on one `SitePickerCard` (gradient accent bar, glowing logo tile, two stat rows, arrow CTA); view counts on internal announcements; `app/site/[siteSlug]/search/page.tsx` written entirely in English behind a navbar link labelled `PENCARIAN`; literal `SJA` string as image fallback.

On the article page: `className="article-content"` at `[articleSlug]/page.tsx:166` matches zero CSS rules in the repo (verified by grep) while the fully-written `.prose-santos` sits unused. `app/[slug]/page.tsx` is ~450 lines of unreachable template behind a `redirect()` at line 100.

Portal tells, limited to two: the 1500ms timer in `SSOAutoSubmit.tsx:26-33` whose own comment says it exists so the user can watch the credential panel; and implementation vocabulary surfaced as UI copy ("(Reroute)", "server-to-server", "Connecting").

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` exit 2, **1 finding** across the whole user-facing set: `layout-transition` at `components/FullscreenHero.tsx:198` (`transition: width` on the hero pager dots). Verified not a no-op — a broader scan returns 2 findings including `app/globals.css:203`. Confirmed no `.impeccable/config.json` suppressing rules (`--no-config` gives identical output). The near-clean result reflects rule coverage in regex/TSX mode, not UI quality: React style objects and Tailwind strings are only partially reachable by its rules.

Detector-adjacent grep evidence, which is where the real signal is:
- inline `style={{`: **230 across 18 site files**, **0 in portal**
- hardcoded hex: **64 across 15 site files**, **0 in portal**
- `focus-visible`: **42 occurrences, all 42 in portal files**, **0 in any public-site component**
- fixed `fontSize: 'NNpx'`: **76 across 15 files**
- `<img>` instead of `next/image`: 5 (4 portal, `SitePickerCard.tsx:86`)

That distribution is the core structural finding: token discipline and focus states exist in exactly the half of the app that the register would have deprioritized.

**Visual overlays.** Not available. No reliable user-visible overlay was produced; no server was started. All rules depending on rendered geometry, computed contrast, or paint timing were never exercised.

## Overall Impression

This is two codebases sharing one URL space. `components/portal/**` is disciplined Tailwind with semantic tokens, `focus-visible`, `aria-*`, `role="group"`, `aria-pressed`, and a genuinely well-designed error-state family. The public brand surfaces are inline style objects with ad-hoc hex, hover behavior implemented as JS DOM mutation, and no focus states at all.

The register expectation is inverted: the PRODUCT-register surface (an internal tool) is the well-crafted one; the BRAND-register surfaces (the newsroom that represents the company to its own workforce) are not. The single biggest opportunity requires no new design — connect the existing, well-documented token layer to the surfaces that ignore it, and delete the duplicates (`HeroSection`, `app/[slug]/page.tsx`, three divergent card implementations).

## What's Working

**The portal's error-state family is a real system, not a set of screens.** `NoCredential`, `CorruptCredential`, `AccessDenied`, plus the two empty states, share one skeleton: 56px tinted icon tile → `font-display` heading → one sentence with the specific app bolded → exactly one action. The severity tint carries meaning (`danger` for denied, `warning` for recoverable), and every recoverable state's button is pre-targeted at the fix (`/portal/credentials?app=${appSlug}`), not a generic settings link.

**`SSOCredentialVault` was designed for the real deployment.** The `execCommand` clipboard fallback (lines 20-44) is commented with the actual internal host and the exact condition (`navigator.clipboard` is secure-context-only) plus a note on when to remove it. `onFocus={(e) => e.currentTarget.select()}` gives keyboard users select-all for free. Three icon buttons, three distinct `aria-label`s. Someone used this before shipping it.

**The token layer is well-engineered and self-documenting.** `globals.css` records *why* each semantic color needs a paired `--*-rgb` channel: without the `<alpha-value>` placeholder Tailwind silently drops every `bg-accent/10` utility, and the comment notes 36 such classes previously compiled to nothing. `tailwind.config.ts` carries a semantic z-index scale with the stacking order spelled out. The tragedy is that the site surfaces consume none of it.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The article body has no typography, and its links are invisible on mobile
`[articleSlug]/page.tsx:165-172` injects CMS HTML into `className="article-content"` — a class with zero matching CSS — plus three inline properties (`17px`, `1.8`, `#e0e0e0`). Global `a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }` applies, and `SiteThemeProvider` only restores link color on `a:not([class]):hover`.

**Why it matters.** Headings inside articles inherit `font-weight: 700` with no margin, so they weld to the paragraph above. Measure runs to 800px ≈ 95 characters, well past the 65-75ch that `.prose-santos`'s own comment specifies. And every in-article hyperlink renders as plain body text, revealed only on hover — which never fires on touch. On the one surface whose entire job is reading, links are undiscoverable for every mobile reader. WCAG 1.4.1.

**Fix.** Swap `article-content` for `prose-santos` and delete the three inline properties; add `text-decoration: underline` to the existing `.prose-santos a` rule. Same class on `app/[slug]/page.tsx`.

### [P0] The article page's back link renders underneath the fixed navbar
`layout.tsx` renders `Navbar` at `position: fixed; zIndex: 200; height: 80px`. `[articleSlug]/page.tsx:114-127` then renders a second `<nav>` at `position: sticky; top: 0; zIndex: 100` containing the only "Kembali ke {site.name}" link. Same origin, lower z-index, shorter. `search/page.tsx` has the identical construction. All values verified in source.

**Why it matters.** The back link is fully occluded and unclickable. A reader arriving from WhatsApp has no in-page route into the newsroom — only the browser back button, which returns them to WhatsApp. It also ships two `<nav>` landmarks per page, one visually dead.

**Fix.** Delete both local `<nav>` blocks; put the back affordance inside `Navbar` (or in `ArticleHero` above the category badge, where its z-index is legitimate).

### [P0] Three listing grids overflow horizontally below 398px
`gridTemplateColumns: "repeat(auto-fill, minmax(350px, 1fr))"` inside a `padding: 0 24px` container at `app/site/[siteSlug]/page.tsx:161`, `app/site/page.tsx:120`, `app/site/[siteSlug]/search/page.tsx:190`. All three confirmed by grep.

**Why it matters.** At 360px viewport the content box is 312px; `minmax(350px, ...)` cannot shrink below 350px. The page scrolls sideways and card text clips. 360px is the modal Android width in Indonesia — so the newsroom, the site picker, and search all break on the most common device.

**Fix.** `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(min(350px, 100%), 1fr))` in all three. One-token edit each, expressible in inline styles.

### [P1] Navbar paints the desktop layout on mobile first paint
`Navbar.tsx:22` `useState(false)` then line 50 `const showDesktopNav = mounted ? isDesktop : true;`. Verified in source. On site pages `customLinks` builds 7 items at `gap: 40px` ≈ 700px inside a 312px container, then snaps to a hamburger after hydration. `globals.css` contains a long comment documenting that this exact `isDesktop`-default-true bug was found and fixed for the admin shell by moving the breakpoint into CSS. The public navbar still has it.

**Fix.** Delete `isDesktop`/`mounted`; render both branches and control visibility with `hidden lg:flex` / `lg:hidden`, the pattern `PortalHeader.tsx:29` already uses.

### [P1] Hover handlers overwrite tokens with hex, permanently
`Navbar.tsx:147-154` starts at `var(--text-secondary)` then `onMouseOut` writes `#a3a3a3`. `Footer.tsx` writes `#333` and `#737373`; `HeroSection.tsx` writes `#404040`. None of those values exist in `globals.css` (`--text-2` is `#A1A1AA`, `--text-3` is `#71717A`), so hovering visibly shifts the color and permanently detaches the element from the token. Because inline styles cannot express `:hover`, every hover state on these surfaces is JS DOM mutation — which is also why none of them respond to keyboard focus (0 `focus-visible` occurrences in the entire public-site component group).

**Fix.** Delete every `onMouseOver`/`onMouseOut` in `Navbar`, `Footer`, `HeroSection`, `SitePickerCard`; replace with `hover:` + `focus-visible:` classes. This fixes hover, focus, and theme support in one change.

### [P1] `HeroSection` positions its controls on top of its own headline on mobile
`aspectRatio: '16 / 9'` at line 143 with content `paddingBottom: 120px`, arrows at `bottom: 120px; right: 48px`, mute at `bottom: 140px`, indicators at `bottom: 60px`. At 360px the section is 202px tall, so all four control clusters land in the upper third, directly over the 28px title and 18px excerpt.

**Fix.** Add a `minHeight` floor and move the four offset clusters into a `globals.css` class with a `@media (max-width: 640px)` block. Better: delete `HeroSection.tsx` and standardize on `FullscreenHero.tsx`, which already uses `clamp()` for every offset.

### [P1] Only 12 articles per site are reachable
`app/site/[siteSlug]/page.tsx:62` — `take: 12`, no `Pagination`, no `page` param. `components/Pagination.tsx` exists and is wired to the near-dead global homepage but not to the actual per-site newsroom.

**Why it matters.** Article 13 is unreachable by browsing. Any announcement older than the twelve most recent effectively ceases to exist on a compliance-adjacent internal-comms surface.

**Fix.** Read `searchParams.page`, add `skip` + a `count()` to the existing `Promise.all`, render `Pagination`. Read `searchParams.category` in the same edit to activate the five dead navbar category links.

### [P1] The SSO relay has no failure state and cannot be skipped
`SSOAutoSubmit.tsx:26-33` waits 1500ms then submits a `className="hidden"` form. No `onError`, no timeout, no visible submit, no cancel. Identical in `SSORerouteSubmit.tsx`.

**Why it matters.** An employee opening four apps each morning pays 6 seconds a day for an animation. Worse: when submission fails (target down, mixed-content block — plausible on an internal IP), the user sees a green check and "Selesai!" while nothing happens. The most confident-looking state in the product is also its silent failure state.

**Fix.** Submit on mount with no delay. After 3000ms without navigation, swap the card to an error state naming the app, with a real visible `<button type="submit">` (the form is real HTML, so it works even with JS broken) and a link back to `/portal`. Wrap status text in `role="status" aria-live="polite"`. Remove the credential panel — once there is no wait, it has no reason to exist.

### [P2] Two design tokens fail WCAG AA at the token level
Computed from the actual values: `--text-3` `#71717A` on `--surface-1` `#111113` = **3.93:1** (used at 12px for `AppCard` category, `AccountSelector` ID, SSO field labels, `portal-login` helper text). White on `--accent` `#ED1C24` = **4.38:1** (every `primary` button and portal CTA at 14px). `text-accent` on `bg-accent-subtle` ≈ **4.12:1** — the active nav item and active filter chip, the two elements whose only job is signalling state. Separately, `#666` on `#0a0a0a` = **3.45:1** is the date/view-count row on every card.

Also: `SiteThemeProvider.tsx:78-84` `getContrastColor()` thresholds perceived brightness `(0.299r + 0.587g + 0.114b)/255 > 0.5`, not WCAG relative luminance, so per-site custom colors get contrast decisions from the wrong equation.

**Fix.** Lighten `--text-3` toward `#8A8A93` (≈4.6:1 on `--surface-1`); use `--brand-red-dark` `#C41920` for primary button backgrounds (white ≈ 6.0:1); add `font-semibold` + `border-accent` to active chips so state isn't carried by a 4.12:1 color alone; replace `getContrastColor` with the relative-luminance formula.

### [P2] Article thumbnails are CSS background images — no alt text, no optimization
Listing (`page.tsx:187`), search, and related-articles grids all render thumbnails as `<div style={{ backgroundImage: url(...) }}>` with no `role` or `aria-label`; `ArticleHero.tsx:154-164` does the same for the lead image.

**Why it matters.** Every article image is absent from the accessibility tree; `background-image` bypasses `next/image` entirely, so full-resolution uploads ship unresized with no `srcset` and no lazy loading into a 180px box; and no reserved ratio means layout shift. `AnnouncementCard.tsx` already does this correctly with `next/image` + `fill` + `alt={title}`, one directory over.

**Fix.** Replace each with `<Image ... alt={announcement.title} fill sizes="(max-width: 640px) 100vw, 350px" />`; `priority` on the `ArticleHero` image since it's the LCP element.

## Persona Red Flags

**Factory/office employee, not tech-savvy, mid-range Android.** First screen after first login is a configuration form — `OnboardingWizard` shows two nested checkbox trees before a single app. "Kredensial disimpan terenkripsi" requires understanding both words; the actual task is "type your HRIS password so the portal can log in for you," which nothing says. Every tap target is under 44px: `Button` md 40px, sm 32px, `Input` 40px, hamburger 36px, "Hapus" ~32px, wizard checkbox rows ~26px — while `PortalHeader`'s mobile links correctly use `min-h-11`, so the right constant is known and applied once. `AccountSelector` distinguishes accounts by raw cuid. A mistyped credential produces no feedback here; it surfaces minutes later in another tab, in another app's wording. `p-8` fixed at every width spends 18% of a 360px viewport on padding.

**Mobile reader arriving from WhatsApp on a slow connection.** `ArticleHero.tsx:60-61` — `height: 85vh; minHeight: 600px`: on 360×640 the 600px floor wins, so the hero is 94% of the viewport and not one word of the announcement is visible without scrolling. The hero autoplays MP4 or a YouTube iframe with no `poster` and no `preload="none"`. The listing fires up to 12 `<video preload="metadata">` requests. Images ship unoptimized (P2). `export const dynamic = "force-dynamic"` on every public page means no CDN caching, and the `viewCount` write is awaited before render — TTFB includes a DB write. The back link is invisible (P0), so the only exit is back to WhatsApp.

**Keyboard / screen-reader user.** The skip link points at `#news`, which exists only in `app/page.tsx` — on every site and article page the first focusable element targets nothing (both verified). Auto-rotating carousels pause on `onMouseEnter` only: a keyboard user has the headline replaced under them every 6 seconds, no pause control (WCAG 2.2.2). Carousel indicators are 4px tall (`FullscreenHero` 8×4, `HeroSection` 24×4), and `HeroSection`'s have no `aria-label` and no `aria-current` while `FullscreenHero`'s do. `Navbar`'s hamburger has no `aria-label` and no `aria-expanded`. `SSOCredentialVault` puts `focus:outline-none` on both readonly inputs, which at specificity (0,2,0) beats the global `*:focus-visible` rule at (0,1,0) — tabbing through the vault shows nothing. Heading order breaks: the three SSO relay components use `<h2>` as page heading on pages with no `<h1>`, while their sibling error states correctly use `<h1>`; `Footer` and `NewsletterSubscribe` use `<h4>` with no `<h3>` above. `FullscreenHero.tsx:118-127` gives the background `role="img" aria-label={current.title}` immediately followed by an `<h1>` with the same text — the headline is announced twice. Form status changes are never announced in `CommentSection` or `NewsletterSubscribe`.

## Minor Observations

- `CorruptCredential` says the same thing twice: "Kredensial rusak. Silakan simpan ulang." then "Kredensial untuk X tidak dapat dibaca. Silakan simpan ulang untuk melanjutkan."
- All seven full-screen portal states use `min-h-screen` inside a layout that already renders a 56px `PortalHeader`, so every "centered" card sits 56px low and the page always scrolls.
- `app/portal/app/[appSlug]/page.tsx` passes `logoPath` to the `REROUTE` branch but not to `AUTO_SUBMIT`, so the most common launch screen always shows a letter tile despite supporting logos.
- Reading time is implemented three times: `[articleSlug]/page.tsx:93`, inside `ArticleHero.tsx`, and `formatReadingTime` in `lib/utils` (used by `ReadingTime.tsx`, imported by nothing on these surfaces).
- `SkeletonCard.tsx` is `height: 192px` while `AnnouncementCard` is `16/10` — if it were ever used, the skeleton would be the wrong shape.
- `AnnouncementCard` sets `color: var(--text-primary)` on admin-editable `category.color` with no contrast validation; a yellow category renders white-on-yellow.
- `pinned` is sorted first at `page.tsx:78` but rendered identically to everything else — the sort is invisible.
- Dead imports on `[articleSlug]/page.tsx`: `FiCalendar`, `FiUser`, `FiEye`, `FiClock`, `format`, `id`.
- Detector's one finding (`transition: width` on 4px absolutely-positioned dots) is technically correct but low-impact; worth fixing as `transform: scaleX()` for correctness, not performance.

## Questions to Consider

- The portal proves this team can write token-driven accessible UI. What would the newsroom look like if it were rebuilt from `components/portal/**`'s conventions instead of from inline styles?
- `app/page.tsx` and `app/site/[siteSlug]` are two homepages; `app/[slug]` and `app/site/[siteSlug]/[articleSlug]` are two article templates. Which two are you actually shipping, and what happens if the other two are deleted rather than maintained?
- If an employee's only job on the portal is opening one app, what is the fastest possible version of that — and how much of the current SSO screen survives it?
- The hero occupies 94% of a phone viewport before any words. Is the lead image confirming the reader is in the right place, or preventing them from reading?
